# Sources and reuse

## Version 2 baseline

The inherited English and Chinese layers arrived as a compiled dictionary. Earlier project history identifies Oxford-based drafting batches and the previous README identifies Oxford University Press, *A Dictionary of Law*, 10th edition (2022), as a reference for some headwords and senses. None of the 8,633 Version 2 senses carried an individual source field. Attribution of a specific sentence to Oxford, or clearance to redistribute it, cannot be established from these records. The 7,696 records without a record-level jurisdiction must not be assigned a jurisdiction by default.

Preserve the baseline and history for audit. Hold new public extracts, bulk exports and training sets drawn from inherited wording pending passage-level provenance or permission. For a flagged sense, compare privately with its documented source, obtain applicable permission or replace it with an independently written definition grounded in a public authority. Record the result at sense level. No commercial source texts or scans are included in this release batch.

## Source-checked senses

The JSONL source object provides the authority, pinpoint, official URL and consultation date. Scope and subject are stored alongside the definition. Follow the link and check the latest text, territorial extent, commencement and exceptions before relying on a definition. For U.S. Code links that identify a public-law snapshot, the consultation date and the date of that snapshot have different meanings.

## Reuse and attribution

Identify the project, commit and stable entry ID, link the licence, note changes and observe share-alike when distributing adaptations. Third-party rights follow their own terms. UK legislative references contain public sector information licensed under the [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/). A court decision or treaty citation identifies the authority for an independently written summary. It gives no general permission to republish third-party annotations, commentary or editorial material.
